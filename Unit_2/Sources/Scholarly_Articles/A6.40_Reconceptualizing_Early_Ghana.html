Selection Overview

McIntosh reassesses Nehemia Levtzion's 1973 account of how Ghana arose, testing it against archaeology on climate change, herder-farmer interaction, trans-Saharan trade, and political organization. She finds no "compelling evidence of regular organized trade" with the Mediterranean before 700 C.E. and argues instead that early Ghana grew out of regional networks moving copper, salt, and foodstuffs between desert and savanna, later dominating the gold trade through alliances, a sacred kingship, and a merchant diaspora. She cautions that the evidence is thin and endorses Roderick McIntosh's model of Ghana as a "slowly consolidated confederation of many small polities" rather than a European-style kingdom.

He proposed that the question of origins and early development of Sudanic polities such as Ghana should be thor- oughly reconsidered with regard to findings from research in archaeology and related disciplines.
