Selection Overview

Forsdyke argues that Herodotus' Histories drew on Athenian polis traditions and so preserves a key tenet of Athenian democratic ideology: that peoples ruled by a tyrant are weak while free citizens are strong. She shows that Herodotus' contrast between Xerxes' whip-driven army and the free Greeks, like the parallel scenes in Aeschylus' Persians, was "derived from the ideology of the Athenian democracy," a contrast she thinks "crystallized only after the experience of the Persian Wars."
