Selection Overview

Creel argues that China pioneered the centralized bureaucratic state, which by Han times was run by officials judged on examinations, records, and merit ratings, and he traces its roots to the hsien, a district governed by an official "appointed by and obedient to the ruler." Although Qin extended the hsien to all of China in 221 B.C.E., he finds the evidence that it began in Qin "decidedly weak" and argues that it more likely originated in the southern state of Chu and spread to Jin, with Qin "a borrower rather than an innovator."
