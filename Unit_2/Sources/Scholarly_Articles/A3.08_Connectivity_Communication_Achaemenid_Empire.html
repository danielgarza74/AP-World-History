Selection Overview

Colburn challenges the common assumption that the Achaemenid Empire's vast size hindered communication, pointing to an extensive road network running from Sardis to Kandahar and Memphis and to the pirradazish courier relay, which he estimates (by comparison with the Pony Express) could carry messages about 230 kilometers a day, far faster than Roman communication. He concludes that either the differences between the two empires need another explanation or "the Achaemenid Empire was more centralized and more integrated than current assumptions allow."

Colburn* Abstract The vast territorial extent of the Achaemenid Empire is often assumed to have impeded connectivity and communication within the empire. This paper challenges the validity of this assumption. Two factors in particular favor this conclusion - the presence of an exten- sive road network and the high communication speed in the empire, made possible by the pirradazish service. Together, they demonstrate the enormous potential for movement and interaction throughout the empire. Keywords Communication speed; connectivity; Achaemenid Empire; pirradazish, postal history, lhe Obstacle of Distance The Achaemenid Empire was among the largest empires of the ancient world. It covered a land area five times greater than any of its predecessors and was matched, later, only by the Xiongnu confederation and the Han Dynasty in China. Although its size is frequently commented upon, the impact of the empire s geography on its overall character and its day-to-day operations has rarely been articulated. Most scholarship assumes implicitly that the empires size inhibited connectivity and communication.
