Gift-Bearing Delegations on the Apadana Stairway, Persepolis
ca. 500–480 B.C.E. (reigns of Darius I and Xerxes I)

Historical Context

Persepolis, in the Persian homeland (Fars, in southwestern Iran), was a ceremonial capital begun by Darius I and continued by Xerxes. Its largest building, the Apadana or audience hall, stood on a raised terrace reached by monumental stairways. The walls of these stairways were carved in low relief with long processions. On one side, rows of Persian and Median nobles and guards advance. On the other, twenty-three delegations from the subject lands climb toward the king, each led by an usher who often holds the first man by the hand. Each group wears its own dress and carries goods associated with its homeland: vessels, textiles, animals, weapons, and gold.

The reliefs are not photographs of a real event. They present an ideal picture of the empire that the king wanted to be seen by those who came to his hall. Scholars debate how to name what the delegations carry: “tribute,” “gifts,” or both. Identifications of individual delegations rest partly on comparison with lists of peoples in royal inscriptions and are not certain in every case. The central audience scene, showing an enthroned king receiving a high official, was later moved from the stairway and is now in the National Museum of Iran; it is not shown here.

Editorial note. The three photographs below show the east stairway as it survives today, weathered and without its original paint. Captions give the identifications commonly proposed for each group.
Primary Source Images
Image 1. Northern wall of the east stairway: a procession of Persian and Median dignitaries. Figures in fluted headdresses and long robes alternate with figures in rounded caps and tunics; a stylized tree stands at left.
Image 2. Southern wall of the east stairway: a delegation usually identified as Lydians (from western Anatolia), bringing vessels, cups, bracelets, and a small chariot drawn by horses (at left). The first man is led by an usher at far left, outside this view.
Image 3. Southern wall of the east stairway: a delegation usually identified as Ionian Greeks, carrying folded textiles, skeins of wool, and objects that may be beehives or bowls.
