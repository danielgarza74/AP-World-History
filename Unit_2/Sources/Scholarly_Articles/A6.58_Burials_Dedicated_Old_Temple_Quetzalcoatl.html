Burials Dedicated to the Old Temple of Quetzalcoatl at Teotihuacan, Mexico
Saburo Sugiyama
 
ARTICLE Burials Dedicated to the Old Temple of Quetzalcoatl at Teotihuacan, Mexico
JOURNAL American Antiquity 54, no. 1 (1989)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 87–88
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/281333
ESSAY PROMPTS 6.4
ARGUMENT ROLES 6.4.1 Supports; 6.4.3 Qualifies
Selection Overview

Sugiyama reports on burials excavated in 1983–84 at the Old Temple of Quetzalcoatl (the Feathered Serpent Pyramid) and, reviewing earlier finds, argues that a great complex of perhaps more than sixty sacrificed people, many of them buried with weapons as "military men, priestly soldiers, or men disguised as military personages," was dedicated to the temple at its construction. He reads this mass sacrifice as a display of the political power of the group that directed the building, and argues that later damage to the temple's sculptures points to "religious and political conflicts" in early Teotihuacan, while noting that no high-status ruler's tomb has yet been found.

This cavity must be older stratigraphically than the floors. However it is probable that the burial was deposited in this cavity during the process of construction of the Old Temple, because only a layer of fill was found between the grave's tepetate floor and the first constructional floor. Furthermore, the location of the burial in relation to the temple strongly suggests that the deposit was a deliberate part of the overall construction plan. The individual interred was a young adult female, approximately 21 to 35 years of age according to general analysis of the bones by Carlos Serrano (personal communication 1985), found in dorsal- flexed position with an east to west orientation. The ulna and radius of both arms were discovered under vertebrae, as if the wrists had been tied behind the back (Figure 3). The only directly associated materials were 1,606 small worked spiral shells, the tips of which were cut, probably to string them as beads of a necklace (see Table 1). Multiple Burial 190 was deposited inside a large, long, east-west pit that measured 8.25 x 1.8 m and was located parallel to the south wall of the temple. The (short) north-south central axis of this pit coincides exactly with the north-south central axis of the Old Temple. Thus it appears very probable that this tomb was planned and executed in connection with the construction of this structure. This is confirmed by stratigraphic evidence. Three pits were excavated to detect the burial and to complement stratigraphic data (Figure 4), and four construction levels were found. The first level consists of a floor and wall faced with typical Teotihuacan concrete (layer XVII of Figure 4, and lower left part of Figure 5), which were cut later to prepare the long grave when the Old Temple was built. This is the only direct architectural evidence that has come to light of a pre- Ciudadela construction, the existence of which previously has been proposed by Cowgill (I1983:326- 327) on the basis of ceramics from surface collections.
