Selection Overview

Sanft argues from the excavated Qin and early Han legal texts of Shuihudi and Zhangjiashan that two changes usually credited to Dong Zhongshu's Confucian practice of deciding cases by the Chunqiu (weighing an offender's intent, and letting relatives hide each other's crimes) grew out of earlier Qin and Han practice. In his reading, citing the classics changed how officials argued about the law more than it changed the law itself.

It is also true. One of the areas in which archaeological discoveries have most improved our understanding is legal history. In recent decades, Shuihudi 目垂虎地(Yunmeng county, Hubei), Zhangjiashan 張家山(Jiangling county, Hubei), and other sites have produced documents that more or less directly represent aspects of the legal systems of the Qin and early Western Han periods.1 These show just how much the early Western Han legal system continued that of the Qin.
