Selection Overview

Wiedemann questions the view that Roman slavery was an "open" system in which a slave could count on being freed almost as a matter of course, arguing from legal texts and wills that owners "paid scant attention to the ideal that a faithful slave should be manumitted." He concludes that masters freed slaves mainly when it served their own interests, while the ideal of regular manumission helped secure slaves' cooperation and let owners "sleep with an easier conscience."
