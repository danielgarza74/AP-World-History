Selection Overview

Li uses Qin administrative and legal manuscripts to show that the newly conquered lands stayed unstable after 221 B.C.E., and that Qin answered with flexible, pragmatic policies, including special laws, tolerant treatment of new subjects, and the transfer of officials, convicts, and soldiers into those lands. He argues that overburdened officials, exploitative extraction, and the resentment of conquered and forcibly relocated people "could, to some extent, explain" why Qin collapsed so quickly.

The inauguration of the Qin empire put an end to centuries of political upheaval and bloody warfare in the Warring States, and China entered a new imperial era. Most of the written information on the process of the Qin unification is found in the “Basic Annals of the First Emperor of Qin” (“Qin Shihuang benji” 秦始皇本紀) from the Shiji 史記.1 This source focuses mainly on the major mil- itary conquests and triumphs of Qin over the territories of the six states leading up to its final unification in 221bce and on its policies on political, economic, and cultural unification and standardization during the imperial period. How- ever, the Shiji offers little information on the sociopolitical conditions of the newly conquered lands after the Qin conquest, the changes experienced by the new subjects, or the governance of these new territories, i.e., the institutional, legal, and social reforms Qin implemented therein. For years, this made further research on this topic almost impossible, despite its importance for elucidating the extraordinary story of the Qin unification. Fortunately, recently discovered texts, especially the legal manuscripts found in Zhangjiashan Han Tomb 張家山漢墓no. 247, those purchased and collected by Yuelu Academy (Yuelu shuyuan 嶽麓書院), and the administra- tive manuscripts found in Liye 里耶Well no. 1, preserve valuable information on the circumstances and administration of Qin’s newly conquered lands.2 As recent studies have shown, they serve as reliable sources for reconstructing 1 Shiji 史記(Beijing: Zhonghua shuju, 1959), 6.223–62. 2 Forthe Ernianlüling二年律令(StatutesandOrdinancesof theSecondYear)materialsinthe
