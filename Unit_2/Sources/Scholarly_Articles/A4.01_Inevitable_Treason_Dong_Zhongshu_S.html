Selection Overview

Arbuckle argues that Dong Zhongshu, long credited (or blamed) with making Confucianism the Han state ideology, was not a loyal servant of the dynasty but an idealist who used a six-part, two-stage historical cycle to predict that the Liu house would soon have to hand the throne to its wisest subject. Drawing on Dong's memorials, his poetry, and his disciple Sui Hong's demand that Emperor Zhao abdicate, he presents Dong as "a subversive" and the earliest known source of the cyclical theories that later aided Wang Mang.

INEVITABLE TREASON: DONG ZHONGSHU'S THEORY OF HISTORICAL CYCLES AND EARLY ATTEMPTS TO INVALIDATE THE HAN MANDATE GARY ARBUCKLE UNIVERSITY OF CALGARY It has been assumed that the most prominent Confucian literatus of Former Han, Dong Zhongshu (c. 195-c. 115 B.C.) devoted himself to the dynasty and its ruling house of Liu, even while trying to restrain them by invoking the power of Heaven. However, reexamination of the evidence shows that Dong was in fact an idealist who was looking beyond the Han to the reign of a new sage-king, em- ploying a six-part, two-stage historical cycle to predict the imminent demise of the Liu and their re- placement by the wisest of their subjects. Such a theory can be attested by references in Dong's memorials to Emperor Wu and in his poetry, and, as well, was attributed to him by a second- generation disciple half a century after his death. It makes him the earliest known advocate of the theories of cyclical history that would culminate in the rise of Wang Mang.
