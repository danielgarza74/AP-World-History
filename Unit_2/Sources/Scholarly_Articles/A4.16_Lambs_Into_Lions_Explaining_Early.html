Selection Overview

Drake questions the long-standing view that Christian coercion of pagans in the fourth century was simply the natural outgrowth of monotheistic intolerance once Constantine opened "the coffers of imperial largesse." He argues that intolerance is a behavior found in every organization, that under Constantine a consensus had grown up "in support of making public life neutral territory," and that Julian's open hostility revived Christian fears of persecution, discrediting moderates and giving militants the upper hand in shaping the church's alliance with the state.
