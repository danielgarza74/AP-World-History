Selection Overview

McCormick and his co-authors, historians and climate scientists, combine tree rings, ice cores, glaciers, cave deposits, lake levels, and written records into a first synthesis of climate across the Roman world from about 100 B.C.E. to 800 C.E. They find that a stable, favorable climate "probably fostered the Empire's unparalleled rise," that this stability "began to dissipate" around 150–200 C.E. as the empire met grave crises, and that renewed instability after 400 coincided with the western collapse, while stressing that their observations are preliminary and "will need considerable amplification and revision."
