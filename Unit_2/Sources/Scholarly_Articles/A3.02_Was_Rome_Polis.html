Selection Overview

Ando examines how Greeks under Roman rule came to terms with it intellectually, arguing that as Greeks turned their loyalties toward Rome, "they required an intellectual model of the empire that could exonerate, even justify, their participation in its political institutions." He traces how Greek writers stretched the language of their own city-states to describe Rome until the empire could be imagined as one great city, so that Greeks "began to share the empire, by first sharing the emperor" well before universal citizenship in 212 C.E.

The observations of Tacitus on Britain and Florus on Spain-that the Romans encouraged the natives to live in cities on level ground, to wear togas, and to speak Latin-are typical of Roman reflection on the topic.' Both Romans and Greeks asserted, however, that Rome's relations with Greece took place on a different level and that between them cultural influence flowed in the opposite direction.
