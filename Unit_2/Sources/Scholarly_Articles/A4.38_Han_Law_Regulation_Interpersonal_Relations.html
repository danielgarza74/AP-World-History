Han Law and the Regulation of Interpersonal Relations: "The Confucianization of the Law" Revisited
Paul R. Goldin
 
ARTICLE Han Law and the Regulation of Interpersonal Relations: "The Confucianization of the Law" Revisited
JOURNAL Asia Major, Third Series 25, no. 1 (2012)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 1–2
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/43486137
ESSAY PROMPTS 4.2, 3.2
ARGUMENT ROLES 4.2.3 Qualifies; 3.2.2 Supports; 4.2.1 Qualifies
Selection Overview

Goldin argues that early Han law, like Qin law, treated law as an administrative tool for the state's interests (the Zhangjiashan statutes repeat Qin statutes almost word for word), but that during the Han, with Dong Zhongshu as the key figure, jurists came to treat law as a tool of moral instruction that protected family obligations even against the state. He also warns that Ch'ü T'ung-tsu's contrast of Confucian ritual with Legalist law oversimplifies the change.
