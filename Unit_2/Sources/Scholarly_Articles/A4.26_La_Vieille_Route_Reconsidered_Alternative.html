La Vieille Route Reconsidered: Alternative Paths for Early Transmission of Buddhism Beyond the Borderlands of South Asia
Jason Neelis
 
ARTICLE La Vieille Route Reconsidered: Alternative Paths for Early Transmission of Buddhism Beyond the Borderlands of South Asia
JOURNAL Bulletin of the Asia Institute 16 (2002)

ARGUMENT ROLES 4.5.2 Supports
Selection Overview

Neelis revisits Alfred Foucher's thesis that Buddhism left South Asia mainly along the "Vieille Route" across the Hindu Kush of Afghanistan. Using petroglyphs, inscriptions, manuscripts, and pilgrims' accounts from the Upper Indus, Gilgit, and Hunza valleys, he argues that more direct routes through the Karakorum linked Gandhāra and Kashmir to the Tarim Basin, so that Buddhist texts, art, and ideas spread through "interconnected capillary routes rather than unidirectional diffusion along a few major arteries," carried by monks, pilgrims, merchants, and other travelers, with élite patronage arriving later.
