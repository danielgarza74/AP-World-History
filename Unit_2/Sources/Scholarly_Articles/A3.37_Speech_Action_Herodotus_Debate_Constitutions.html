Speech and Action: Herodotus' Debate on the Constitutions
Christopher Pelling
 
ARTICLE Speech and Action: Herodotus' Debate on the Constitutions
JOURNAL Proceedings of the Cambridge Philological Society 48 (2002)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 123–124

ESSAY PROMPTS 3.1
ARGUMENT ROLES 3.1.1 Qualifies
Selection Overview

Pelling reads Herodotus' debate among Persian nobles over democracy, oligarchy, and monarchy (Histories 3.80–82) as a deliberately "Greek" scene embedded in its narrative, arguing that Herodotus uses it to unsettle Greek stereotypes: while "'tyranny' remains one of the firmest markers of Persia," the episode suggests "that Greeks were mistaken in their stereotype of Persians." He concludes that "a straightforward us-and-them polarity is inadequate," since Persian kings differ from one another and Greek freedom could also produce tyranny.
