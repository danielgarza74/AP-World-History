Selection Overview

Witzel compares the language, place names, archaeology, and named kings of the late Vedic and earliest Buddhist texts and concludes that a real gap in time separates the two bodies of texts. He describes the Buddha's eastern lands as a distinct region that was only recently and partly "Sanskritized." Against Bronkhorst, however, he dates the oldest Upaniṣads before the Buddha and traces the karma–rebirth complex to late Vedic thought.

Both text corpora have still not been explored sufficiently well as to establish either the beginning of actual Buddhist text formation, or the end of Vedic text formation and the closure of the Vedic corpus.2 This has recently emphasized by Oskar von Hinüber: "... early Buddhist historiography ... is deplorably absent ..." and later, "our sources never allow us to go beyond more or less likely or probable conclusions about the roots of the texts that reach far back into period of early Buddhism", adding the same caveat for the date of the closure of a text.3 As a further caveat, it must be underlined that I leave aside, for the most part, the development of thought, philosophy and religion. It remains a difficult undertaking to trace their multiple strands, impossible to carry out in a brief paper.4 Rather, I will concentrate on some aspects of archaeology, material culture, language, society and contemporary history.
