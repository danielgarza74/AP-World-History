Selection Overview

Schopen examines rules in the Mūlasarvāstivāda-vinaya, an Indian Buddhist monastic code, that direct monks to accept permanent endowments, lend them out at interest, and record loans in written contracts, rules closely parallel to classical Indian legal texts. He argues that the code presents these practices as a response to lay donors' concern that their monasteries be maintained after their deaths and keep producing merit for them, and that such rules presuppose "permanent, well-organized, and fully institutionalized monasteries" deeply engaged in economic life.

DOING BUSINESS FOR THE LORD: LENDING ON INTEREST AND WRI'TTEN LOAN CONTRACTS IN THE MULASARVASTIVADA-VINAYA GREGORY SCHOPEN UNIVERSITY OF TEXAS The Mulasarvastivdda-vinaya, a Buddhist monastic code, contains a wealth of little-studied ma- terial bearing on the institutional, economic and legal history of monastic Buddhism in India. It ap- pears, moreover, to have close links with classical dharmasdstra or Indian legal literature. The rules it contains in regard to lending on interest and written loan contracts, for example, are remarkably close to those found in the Yajnavalkyasmrti. The account of the promulgation of these rules is also of interest. It suggests how monks understood the long-term concerns of donors, and how they justified their financial activities in terms of the religious needs of those donors. The presence of such rules in a Buddhist monastic code, finally, presupposes the existence of permanent, well-orga- nized, and fully institutionalized monasteries which can be approximately dated in the archeological record. Such confirmation from an extant Sanskrit text is, of course, welcome, but perhaps a more important point is that without the work of sinologists the significance of the Civara-vastu passage might very easily be missed. Scholars working on China have in fact very often been the first to introduce and make available impor- tant Indian material bearing on the institutional and economic history of Buddhism, but this material rarely, or never, makes it into Indian studies. References to Gernet's Les Aspects economiques du bouddhisme, for example, are extremely rare in works on Indian cul- tural and economic history. D. D.
