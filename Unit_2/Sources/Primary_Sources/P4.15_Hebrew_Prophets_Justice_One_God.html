Hebrew Prophets on Justice and One God: Amos, Micah, and Isaiah 45
Amos and Micah, 8th century B.C.E.; Isaiah 45, ca. 550–539 B.C.E.; the books were gathered and edited later
 
TOC FIT Toward Monotheism: The Search for God in the Middle East—Judaism (textbook pp. 157–159).
WHY HERE Prophets who put justice above sacrifice, and one who says that a single God rules all nations, including the Persian king Cyrus: Hebrew evidence for a moral and universal God, tied to particular crises.
TREATMENT Public-domain translation (World English Bible), three selections: Amos 5:10–15, 21–24; Micah 6:6–12; Isaiah 45:1–7, 22–23. Later edited books.

ARGUMENT ROLES 4.1.1 Supports; 4.1.2 Supports; 4.1.3 Qualifies
Historical Context

The Hebrew Bible’s “prophets” were spokesmen who said they brought the message of Yahweh, the God of Israel, to kings and people. Amos, a herdsman from Judah, spoke in the northern kingdom of Israel about the middle of the eighth century B.C.E.; Micah spoke in Judah a little later. Both attack wealthy men who take bribes, cheat in trade, and think that sacrifices make them right with God. The third passage is from a different age. After the Babylonians destroyed Jerusalem in 586 B.C.E. and took many of its leaders to Babylon, as most scholars hold, an unnamed prophet in the Book of Isaiah (chapters 40–55, often called Second Isaiah) addressed the exiles about 550–539, as the Persian king Cyrus advanced on Babylon. He calls Cyrus Yahweh’s “anointed” and says that there is no God besides Yahweh (45:5).

These books reached their present form later: scribes collected the oracles, arranged them, and sometimes added to them, and scholars disagree about which words go back to the prophets themselves. Read them for the claims they make about what God requires, whom God rules, and how those claims arise from particular circumstances: the wealth and injustice of the eighth-century kingdoms, and exile under foreign empires in the sixth century. The passages are a window into one tradition’s ideas, not a record of what most Israelites believed or did.

Editorial note. The text is the World English Bible (WEB), a public-domain modern English translation, as served by Bible Gateway (retrieved October 2, 2026). Verse numbers are the small bold numbers; line breaks follow the translation’s poetry. The classic WEB, which is the edition on Bible Gateway and at eBible.org/eng-web, renders the name of God as “Yahweh”; eBible.org’s updated editions print “LORD.” Because the selections are excerpts, some quotation marks are unpaired: the closing quotation mark at the end of Amos 5:15 belongs to a speech that begins at 5:4, which is not printed. Amos 5

10 They hate him who reproves in the gate,
and they abhor him who speaks blamelessly.

11 Therefore, because you trample on the poor and take taxes from him of wheat, you have built houses of cut stone, but you will not dwell in them.
You have planted pleasant vineyards,
but you shall not drink their wine.

12 For I know how many are your offenses,
and how great are your sins—
you who afflict the just,
who take a bribe,
and who turn away the needy in the courts.

13 Therefore a prudent person keeps silent in such a time,
for it is an evil time.

14 Seek good, and not evil,
that you may live;
and so Yahweh, the God of Armies, will be with you,
as you say.

15 Hate evil, love good,
and establish justice in the courts.
It may be that Yahweh, the God of Armies, will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph.”

[. . .]

21 I hate, I despise your feasts,
and I can’t stand your solemn assemblies.

22 Yes, though you offer me your burnt offerings and meal offerings,
I will not accept them;
neither will I regard the peace offerings of your fat animals.

23 Take away from me the noise of your songs!
I will not listen to the music of your harps.

24 But let justice roll on like rivers,
and righteousness like a mighty stream.

Micah 6

6 How shall I come before Yahweh,
and bow myself before the exalted God?
Shall I come before him with burnt offerings,
with calves a year old?

7 Will Yahweh be pleased with thousands of rams?
With tens of thousands of rivers of oil?
Shall I give my firstborn for my disobedience?
The fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?

8 He has shown you, O man, what is good.
What does Yahweh require of you, but to act justly,
to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?

9 Yahweh’s voice calls to the city—
and wisdom fears your name—
“Listen to the rod,
and he who appointed it.

10 Are there yet treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked,
and a short ephah that is accursed?

11 Shall I tolerate dishonest scales,
and a bag of deceitful weights?

12 Her rich men are full of violence,
her inhabitants speak lies,
and their tongue is deceitful in their speech.

Isaiah 45

1 Yahweh says to his anointed, to Cyrus, whose right hand I have held to subdue nations before him and strip kings of their armor, to open the doors before him, and the gates shall not be shut:

2 “I will go before you
and make the rough places smooth.
I will break the doors of bronze in pieces
and cut apart the bars of iron.

3 I will give you the treasures of darkness
and hidden riches of secret places,
that you may know that it is I, Yahweh, who calls you by your name,
even the God of Israel.

4 For Jacob my servant’s sake,
and Israel my chosen,
I have called you by your name.
I have given you a title,
though you have not known me.

5 I am Yahweh, and there is no one else.
Besides me, there is no God.
I will strengthen you,
though you have not known me,

6 that they may know from the rising of the sun,
and from the west,
that there is no one besides me.
I am Yahweh, and there is no one else.

7 I form the light
and create darkness.
I make peace
and create calamity.
I am Yahweh,
who does all these things.

[. . .]

22 “Look to me, and be saved, all the ends of the earth;
for I am God, and there is no other.

23 I have sworn by myself.
The word has gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and will not be revoked, that to me every knee shall bow,
every tongue shall take an oath.
